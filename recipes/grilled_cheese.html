Grilled Cheese

Description

A classic grilled cheese is made using white or sourdough bread, butter or mayonnaise, and melting cheeses like American, cheddar, or Gruyère.

The modern American grilled cheese sandwich originated in the 1920s, driven by the commercial availability of two key ingredients: cheap processed cheese and factory pre-sliced white bread. While combining cooked bread and cheese dates back to ancient Rome, the specific sandwich we eat today was a product of 20th-century industrial innovation.

Ingredients

  • 2 slices sourdough bread
  • Mayonnaise
  • Dijon mustard
  • 1 to 2 ounces grated sharp cheddar cheese, depending on the size of your bread
  • 1 to 2 ounces grated Gruyère or raclette cheese, depending on the size of your bread
  • Butter, for the pan

Steps

  1. Place the bread slices on a cutting board and spread the top side with a thin layer of mayo. Flip one slice of bread and spread its other side with Dijon mustard. Layer the cheddar and Gruyère or Raclette cheeses on top of the mustard, then place the other slice of bread on top of the cheese, mayo side out.
  2. Heat a nonstick or cast-iron skillet over medium-low heat and melt enough butter in the bottom of the pan to coat it. Place the sandwich in the pan, cover, and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, or until the bottom slice of bread is golden brown and crisp. Flip, replace the lid, and cook until the other slice of bread is golden brown and the cheese is melted, 1 to 3 minutes. Reduce the heat to low if the bread is getting too brown before the cheese is fully melted.
  3. Slice and serve.
